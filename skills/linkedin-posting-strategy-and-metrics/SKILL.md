# LinkedIn posting strategy and metrics

Posting well is half the job. The other half is choosing the audience, the schedule and the measures, then learning from what happens. This skill sets that up.

## Workflow

1. **Set the goal and audience first.** Ask what the user wants from LinkedIn (leads, hiring, reputation, community) and who exactly must see the posts. A post that draws 50 comments from prospects beats 500 from strangers. Virality is a means, not the aim.
2. **Choose a content mix.** Rotate formats from `linkedin-post-templates` (story, how-to, list, opinion, occasional offer). Lean B2B expertise (how-tos, case studies, stories of problems solved) for decision-makers, and personal narrative for a broad creator brand.
3. **Set cadence and timing.**
   - A sustainable 2 to 4 posts a week beats bursts. Consistency compounds because each hit grows the baseline audience.
   - Default slot: Tuesday to Thursday, about 9 to 11 AM in the main audience time zone. Monday is acceptable; avoid late Friday. Weekends can suit reflective personal posts with less competition.
   - Treat this as a starting point and test against the user's own analytics, since audiences differ.
4. **Plan the first hour.** Early engagement velocity strongly shapes reach in the source analysis. Schedule time to be present: reply to every early comment thoughtfully, ask follow-up questions, welcome polite disagreement. Do not recruit friends to inflate numbers or join engagement pods; both risk platform penalties and draw shallow interaction.
5. **Handle comment-to-get posts well.** Prepare delivery before posting (a DM template, a hosted file), reply to each commenter, and be clear if there is a follow-up or opt-in step.
6. **Measure and iterate** with `references/metrics-loop.md`: log each post, compute engagement rate, find what repeats, change one variable at a time.
7. **Review monthly.** Double down on top formats and topics, drop consistent flops, and check that the right people are engaging.

## Findings to use (with confidence)
| Finding | How to use it | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Personal, subjective voice outperforms impersonal | Write with "I" and "you", add your take | Medium-high (consistent across sources) |
| Posts without external links reach more | Put links in comments or DMs | Medium-high |
| First hour of engagement matters | Be present, reply fast | Medium |
| Higher comment-to-reaction ratio marks standout posts | Ask a real question, reply to commenters | Medium |
| Mid-week morning posting | Default slot, then test | Medium-low (varies by audience) |
| Longer posts (1,000 to 2,000 characters) can win | Only if value density holds | Medium-low |
| Specific headline numbers (for example 31.7% subjectivity effect) | Do not quote as fact | Low (see evidence notes) |

## Guardrails
- Do not promise virality. A viral post is a small-probability event; the controllable part is improving the average.
- Do not manufacture engagement (pods, pumped likes, fake scarcity, bait like "comment yes if you agree" without a purpose).
- Do not copy hooks or whole posts from other creators; borrow structure, not text.
- Do not exploit commenters: no spam DMs, no pitching people who only asked for a free resource.
- Treat the source report's statistics as illustrative; read `references/evidence-notes.md` before citing any number to a client.
