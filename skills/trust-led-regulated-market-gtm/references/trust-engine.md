# Trust engine details (paraphrased from the Rogo teardown)
Product: an agent that does analyst work (screening deals, comparable analyses, pitch drafts, confidential information memoranda) inside Excel, PowerPoint, Word and the firm's data warehouse; enterprise software on multi-year contracts; widely cited outside estimate of about $3,300 per seat per year (not disclosed by the company).
History: founded 2021-22 by ex-bankers; first customers late 2023; seed in February 2024 after traction; ARR about $2M (2024), $15M+ (2025), $50M+ (2026); valuation $350M (Apr 2025), $750M (Jan 2026), ~$2B (Apr 2026). Seven-figure ARR in under five months with one rep.

## Plays
1. **Sell trust**: click-through audit trail; hover a number to highlight the page, paragraph and cell in the source filing. Many LLM wrappers died within months because "usually right" fails under zero-error tolerance. Don't copy if buyers tolerate mistakes (then compete on speed or output, as Cursor did).
2. **Win the hardest name first**: top banks give credibility to everyone beneath them. Don't copy in flat markets.
3. **Put insiders in the customer's domain**: founders sold peer to peer; the forward-deployed banker model (roughly half the company are former finance professionals) embeds, onboards from analyst to managing director, calibrates output to house style, routes gaps to product. Demo on the buyer's own work. Don't copy if a self-serve trial sells the product.
4. **Let buyers vouch**: investors from the customer base and a senior hire from the profession. Don't copy if buyers ignore who stands behind you.
5. **Clear the compliance gate**: single-tenant deployment; SOC 2, ISO; alignment with the EU AI Act; gets past InfoSec reviews that most AI startups fail.

## The first door
Founders' network opened the first meetings (an introduction years earlier to a future CEO of a major bank). It cannot be copied, but every trust engine needs a first door: inherited, manufactured (Harvey's cold email to a lab became an investment) or bought through investors and senior hires. A warm introduction buys one hearing; the other 300 accounts bought because the output held up and peers had signed.

## Four conditions (hold in both Harvey and Rogo)
Cost of being wrong is career-ending; buyer is a prestige hierarchy; buying runs on relationships and reputation; work valuable enough to pay a premium for output someone will stake their name on.

## Risks named by the author
Services-versus-software margin question; commoditisation by frontier models and bundling by data vendors; large banks building in-house; seat pricing paradox (better automation means fewer analyst seats); hollowing out the junior apprenticeship pipeline.
