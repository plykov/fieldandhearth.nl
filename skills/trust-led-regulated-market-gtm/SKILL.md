# Trust-Led GTM for High-Stakes Professions (Rogo / Harvey pattern)

Based on the Origin Notes Rogo teardown (2026), paraphrased. Facts are mostly company-reported: Rogo's AI agent runs at 300+ institutions with 35,000+ users, ARR past $50M and a ~$2B valuation (mid-2026). Use as illustration only.

Companions: `gtm-tactic-library`, `strategic-selling-blue-sheet`, `selling-with-perspective`, `meddicc-deal-qualification`, `bottom-up-to-enterprise-gtm` (for the opposite case).

## Workflow
1. **Test the four conditions** (`references/trust-engine.md`): cost of error career-ending; buyer is a prestige hierarchy; buying runs on relationships and reputation; work valuable enough for premium pricing. If fewer than three hold, prefer a product-superiority or bottom-up engine.
2. **Design verifiability into the product**: every figure traceable to its source in one click; outputs a senior person can check in seconds.
3. **Clear the compliance gate before selling**: security review pack, certifications, single-tenant or equivalent isolation, regulatory alignment.
4. **Pick the first marquee name** and the first door (inherited, manufactured or bought); plan how a warm introduction turns into evidence.
5. **Build the insider motion**: founders who sell peer to peer, then forward-deployed experts who configure the product on the buyer's own work and feed gaps back.
6. **Use peer proof**: reference programmes, investor and adviser overlap with the customer base, senior hires from the profession.
7. **Plan the economics**: services content, seat pricing versus work automated, commoditisation by foundation models, bundling by incumbents.

## Output template
Condition test | verifiability features | compliance gate checklist | marquee-logo plan and first door | insider/forward-deployed plan | peer-proof plan | pricing and margin risks | metrics (time to security approval, adoption per seat, expansion by team level).

## Guardrails
- Verifiable output is a promise: never claim accuracy you cannot back; keep humans accountable for high-stakes decisions.
- Investor-as-customer overlap and logos must be disclosed honestly; do not imply endorsement that does not exist.
- Respect customers' confidentiality and regulatory duties; automation that thins junior ranks has workforce consequences worth discussing openly.
