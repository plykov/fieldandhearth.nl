# Pilot design and checklist (paraphrased)

## SaaStr's roster (illustrative)
Inbound chat agent; warm-outbound agent; net-new account agent; agent for website visitors and ghosted prospects; a self-built coordinating "VP of Marketing" agent. A human negotiates.

## Readiness checklist
[ ] A sales playbook that already works with humans | [ ] A best rep whose workflow can be written down | [ ] Lead volume that humans cannot cover | [ ] 30 days of daily training capacity per agent | [ ] Two owners at 15-20 hours a week each | [ ] Budget (SaaStr cites $500K+ of infrastructure) | [ ] Data/privacy review | [ ] Clear escalation and negotiation rules | [ ] A way to audit transcripts daily.

## B leads
Mid-sized prospects, overnight chats and deals that went quiet. Humans avoid them; agents don't mind. Reported as valuable "if you add AI".

## Where it breaks (author's and founder's caveats)
- Maybe the market (AI vendors with money to spend) did the work, not the agents; repeatability is unproven.
- Agents hallucinate, need daily review, write outreach lacking a strong rep's texture.
- Large, six-figure deals to buyers who don't know you need a human; unless the buyer is also an agent. Ready-to-buy inbound with a strong brand can be picked up by an agent.
- Don't copy if you've never had a rep who could sell the product, or close ~40 large deals a year: an agent copies your best salesperson and adds coverage.

## 30-day pilot sketch
Week 1: pick one job and one lead pool; write the script from the best rep's calls; set guardrails. Weeks 2-3: shadow mode (draft only), daily review, fix failure patterns. Week 4: limited live volume with human escalation; compare meetings and reply quality with a human control group. Decide expand, fix, or stop.
