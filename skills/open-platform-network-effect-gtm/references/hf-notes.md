# Hugging Face notes (paraphrased)
Revenue sources: individual and team subscriptions ($9-$20 a month at the time), compute billed per hour on rented GPUs, and enterprise private platform (private model hosting, on-prem, security, audit logs, compliance).
## Phase 1 (2016-2022): build the platform
- **Give away the on-ramp**: the Transformers library made using models a few lines of code; it became the default research tool and led users to the Hub. Same open-source-substrate move as a framework that pulls developers to a host. Don't copy if your tool won't become the standard.
- **Host everyone's work**: Hub for models, datasets and demos (Spaces); more uploads drew more downloads and vice versa; a community can't be bought. Needs early timing and neutrality; don't copy if you can't stay neutral.
- **Delay the money**: revenue began 2021; adoption first. Needs patient capital and low-burn hosting.
## Phase 2 (2023-2026): open-model wave
Closed models looked far ahead in 2023, then open models became credible and matched frontier quality by 2026, lifting the hub. Plays:
- **Enterprise deal** arrived when companies wanted open models hosted privately; bottom-up first.
- **Scoreboard**: downloads and trending cited as the adoption signal; an open-model leaderboard became the reference until it was gamed. Another company owns the usage scoreboard for inference.
- **Replay on new ground**: robotics library and datasets (datasets from ~1,100 to 27,000 in a year). Don't copy if an entrenched hub exists.
## Questions underneath
Strongest distribution, weakest monetization: it sits at discovery and comparison, cloud-agnostic, while clouds host production on their own stacks. The acquirer (a chip company) gains an open-model ecosystem that keeps GPU demand high and a counterweight to closed labs. Can it stay neutral once owned?
