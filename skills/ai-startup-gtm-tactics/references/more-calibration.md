# Extra calibration for the four tactics (paraphrased from later Origin Notes teardowns)

## Launch as a campaign (Supabase)
Since March 2021: one launch a day for a week, every few months. Features ship when ready; the week is when they are announced; the engineer who built each writes the post; planned schedule across forums, launch sites, social and chat communities. CEO reports about 20% bumps each time. Precondition: enough real shipped work and a watching audience. Don't copy if you would hold back features or pad the week.

## Freemium conversion arithmetic (Supabase)
250K+ paying customers (June 2026) against 13M+ registered developers (Oct 2026) is roughly 2 in 100 by the author's arithmetic (registered accounts, not active users). Use it to size how much free usage a plan must carry; fund the gap (the company raised $650M in 2026 and agreed to buy a small-database technology firm to run many small databases cheaply).

## Usage pricing and sales (Replit, Supabase)
Move from per-seat to platform fee plus prepaid credits for companies; keep self-serve to a contract ceiling; trigger human help from usage signals.

## Founder-led social, extended
Gamma: the founder made demo content himself before paying creators. SaaStr: daily publishing since 2012 (2,250+ answers on a Q&A site, 1M followers by 2020). Lenny: weekly for six years. Common precondition: operating credibility and a multi-year cadence. Counter-sign: announcements only, or stopping after months.

## Marketplaces and partners, extended
The series' other channel pattern is agencies delivering the product to clients. Marketplace listings help purchasing but do not create demand.

## Pricing risk note (Rogo)
Seat pricing for a product that automates the seat-holder's work shrinks the seat base as the product improves; consider usage or outcome-based components.
