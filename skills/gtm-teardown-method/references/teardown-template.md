# Teardown template and checks (paraphrased from the article's structure)

## Section skeleton
1. **Hook**: a surprising before/after (layoffs and missed target, then rapid growth).
2. **What the company actually is**: two or three plain sentences for outsiders (skip-able block).
3. **Numbers that prove the playbook works**: starting point, ARR milestones with dates, latest figures, targets, users, margins, funding, outside estimates. Label sources.
4. **How they got stuck**: founding story in brief, first market, product that did not change the user base.
5. **The engine**: who they now build for and why; a worked example of the user journey; the customer story; the bottom line that one tactic is the engine and others support it.
6. **Tactics (each)**: The tactic. The number. The precondition. Don't copy if. Who else runs the same tactic (cross-references to other teardowns).
7. **After the first wave**: how the sales team formed, ratios, self-serve ceiling, a quote from an investor or customer.
8. **How much was timing?** What would have happened without the market shift; what the company owned and earned (for example years of infrastructure).
9. **The question underneath**: structural risks (suppliers becoming competitors, margin, churn) and what next year tests.
10. **Sources** with dates and links; note that the company publishes milestones, not full financials.

## Source labels
(C) company or executive statement | (P) press report | (E) outside estimate | (Q) quoted second-hand. Prefer two independent sources for key numbers; show contradictions (for example some reports give the same figure as annualised revenue and others as full-year revenue).

## Tactic card (reusable)
Tactic | Number | Precondition | Don't copy if | Who else runs it | Evidence quality.

## Timing test
Would a competitor with the same market opening have won? Which assets pre-existed (infrastructure, distribution, data, brand)? What was luck, what was choice? Which parts transfer to a company without those assets?

## Steal / adapt / skip
For each tactic in the user's context: steal (preconditions met), adapt (needs a changed precondition), skip (counter-sign present), plus the first small experiment to run.
