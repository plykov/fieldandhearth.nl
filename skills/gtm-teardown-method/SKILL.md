# GTM Teardown Method

Reverse-engineered from the structure and standards of the Origin Notes "Replit GTM playbook" teardown (2026). Use it to analyse any company's go-to-market and to decide what is portable. Paraphrased for planning use.

Companion skills: `bottom-up-to-enterprise-gtm`, `ai-startup-gtm-tactics`, `business-strategy-and-startups`, `position-b2b-tech`, `consulter-mind-problem-solving`.

## Workflow
1. **Define the question**: why this company, what puzzle (for example a company stuck for years, then 100x in a year).
2. **Gather sources** and tag each claim by type (`references/teardown-template.md`): company/executive statement, press report, outside estimate, second-hand quote. Record dates.
3. **Write the numbers section** with the disclosed figures, flagged by source quality and what is not disclosed.
4. **Explain the stall and the turn**: who they built for, what changed (model, market, customer), what they stopped doing.
5. **Name the engine** (the one tactic the rest support) and the supporting tactics. For each: tactic, number, precondition, don't copy if, who else runs it.
6. **Describe what comes after the first wave**: sales team, motion, ratios.
7. **Run the timing test**: how much was market momentum vs infrastructure or capability the company had earned.
8. **State the open question** (risks, dependence, what next year tests) and list sources.
9. Output the teardown and a one-page "steal / adapt / skip" summary for the user's context.

## After the teardown
Add each tactic as a card to `gtm-tactic-library` (same fields), classify the engine family (see that skill's `references/engines.md`), and when a company's growth rests on narrative or incentives rather than traction use `references/narrative-and-incentive-analysis.md`.

## Output template
Title and one-line thesis | what the company is | numbers table with source labels | how they got stuck | the engine and four to five tactics (five fields each) | what changed after the first wave | timing assessment | underlying question | steal/adapt/skip | sources.

## Guardrails
- Do not present unverified figures as facts; label company-reported claims, estimates and second-hand quotes, and say which numbers are not disclosed.
- Attribute quotes and keep them short; do not reproduce paywalled or copyrighted content at length.
- Avoid survivorship bias: one success story proves a pattern is possible, not that it will work for the user.
