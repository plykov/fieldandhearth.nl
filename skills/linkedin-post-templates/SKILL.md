# LinkedIn post templates

Turn an idea, story or expertise into a finished LinkedIn post. The templates are scaffolds: the quality comes from the user's real details, so collect those before filling anything in.

## Workflow

1. **Intake (ask only what is missing):** the one point of the post, who it is for, the real story or proof behind it (numbers, names, dates), and the goal (reach, trust, leads, discussion).
2. **Pick the type** with the table below. State the choice and why in one line.
3. **Fill the skeleton** from `references/templates.md`. Use the user's facts. If a detail is missing, leave a clear placeholder rather than inventing it.
4. **Write the hook last** or hand it to `linkedin-hook-and-rehook-writer`. The opener decides the tap, so give it its own pass.
5. **Run the checklist** and fix failures.
6. Deliver the post, one alternate hook, and one suggestion for the first-hour comment reply plan if the user wants it.

## Pick the type
| Situation | Type |
|---|---|
| A real setback or turning point with a lesson | Personal story with lesson (template 1) |
| A process or method you can teach in steps | How-to educational (template 2) |
| Several short observations from experience | Listicle of insights (template 3) |
| A view that cuts against common belief and you can back it | Contrarian opinion (template 4) |
| A genuinely useful free asset and capacity to deliver it | Lead magnet offer (template 5) |
| A hot topic where you can add your own take | Trend commentary: use template 4 with a timely hook |
| A question your network can answer | Engagement prompt: give your own answer first |
| A compact principle you can back with one example | Mindset insight (see body-structures.md) |
| An experiment or client result with figures | Challenge-and-results case study (see body-structures.md) |
Blend types freely, for example a story that ends with a short list of lessons.

## Style rules (why each matters)
- **Write like you talk.** First and second person ("I", "you") signal a human; corporate voice reads as an ad.
- **One to three sentences per paragraph**, white space between. Mobile readers abandon walls of text, and length is fine when it looks easy.
- **Specifics beat adjectives:** "$0 revenue for six months" over "a long time".
- **Plain words.** Aim for a school-age reading level; replace buzzwords and explain unavoidable terms.
- **No external link in the body.** Put links in the first comment or share via DM. The source analysis found link posts reach less.
- **Positive close.** Even a post about failure should end on a lesson or way forward.
- **End with a real question or invitation** tied to the post's point, not generic engagement bait.
- 2 to 5 relevant hashtags at most, mentions only when meaningful.
- Length: roughly 1,000 to 2,000 characters is a comfortable range; earn every line.

## Pre-publish checklist
- [ ] First 200 characters create a reason to tap see more
- [ ] One clear point, one clear reader
- [ ] Real specifics (numbers, names, dates), none invented
- [ ] Short paragraphs, no jargon
- [ ] A lesson or value the reader can use
- [ ] No link in the body, a closing question or invitation
- [ ] Tone is generous, not boastful; no claim the user cannot support

## Guardrails
- Never fabricate a story, metric, client or quote to fit a template. Ask, or mark the gap.
- Lead magnet posts: deliver what you promise to every commenter, and do not invent urgency or scarcity. See `references/templates.md`, template 5, and use `lead-magnet-playbook` for building the asset.
- Do not coach engagement pods or asking friends to pump a post; see `linkedin-posting-strategy-and-metrics`.

Read `references/templates.md` for the five skeletons with examples, and `references/body-structures.md` for body shapes, the hook-to-payoff contract and a sixth format (inspirational mindset insight). Read `references/evidence-notes.md` before quoting any statistic from the source analysis.
