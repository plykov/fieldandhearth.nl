# After the first wave: adding sales (paraphrased)
Bottom-up reached large accounts with about four sales reps; inbound then outran them (one person handled enterprise conversations on top of other jobs). A senior revenue leader joined in late 2025 and the plan was to grow from roughly 40 to over 200 go-to-market staff within a year (self-reported).
How the motion works now:
- **Land in one team, then expand**: get a division "ultra successful", then move to the next.
- **A product advocate for every customer**: onboarding, retention and expansion, with field engineers before and after the sale.
- **Small books**: about 30-40 accounts per commercial rep; about 10-20 enterprise customers per rep.
- **Self-serve for smaller deals**: companies can buy enterprise contracts up to about $200,000 inside the product; larger deals go through sales.
- Self-serve and sales are two stages of one engine: the product starts the employee, the employee creates demand inside the company, people handle security review, procurement, onboarding and expansion.
- A well-known investor's headline: everyone ends up with a sales team; in the AI era it just scales later.
Hiring triggers to test in your own data: inbound enterprise requests unanswered; deals stuck in security/procurement; expansion needing coordination across teams; need for implementation help; customers asking for a named contact.
Design questions: self-serve ceiling (contract size where a human is required)? advocate/CS ratio? field engineering for security questionnaires? incentives that keep reps from slowing self-serve?
