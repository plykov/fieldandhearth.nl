# Preconditions, evidence and risks (from the Replit teardown, paraphrased)

## Facts to reason from (company-reported unless noted; 2024-2026)
- Replit spent years near $2.8M revenue selling to schools and to programmers (its first AI product, Ghostwriter, still required knowing how to code).
- The turn: in 2024 it cut about half its staff and refocused on non-technical knowledge workers; the Agent (September 2024, built on a frontier model) made building software possible by describing it.
- Reported growth: roughly $10M ARR at end of 2024, over $100M in June 2025, $150M annualised by September 2025; later claims go higher (up to $250M and a $1B run-rate target for end of 2026); an outside estimate was about $525M ARR in April 2026. 50M+ registered accounts; users at 85% of the Fortune 500; net retention "300% in some cases"; enterprise gross margins "80% to 90%"; most revenue still from individual subscribers per a press report. Use only as context.
- Typical use: internal tools (dashboards, SQL query tools, Jira views). At one customer more than 80% of users had little technical experience and it spread from one team to the whole organisation.
- Enterprise logos (for example large internet and software companies) came through employees adopting then asking to buy, not outbound.

## Preconditions
1. A user can buy and get value alone in about a day, at a price a manager can put on a work card, with no approval.
2. An enterprise layer exists that is worth paying for (centralised billing, controls, security answers).
3. The product does not need a technical person to deliver value.
4. You can answer a security team's questions before the deal stalls (data handling, permissions, environments, incident response).
5. Guardrails protect users who cannot read what the product built.
Counter-signs ("don't copy if"): needs a technical person; no credible answers for security; usage costs per heavy user exceed what a cheap plan collects.

## Risks to monitor
- Retention and churn among non-technical users are not published; ask for cohort data in your own business.
- Margin: usage-based AI costs; reported gross margin swung negative to positive within a year.
- Supplier risk: the model providers also sell competing agents; defend with platform depth (hosting, databases, security, enterprise controls, sales).
- Concentration: individual subscriptions can dominate revenue even when enterprise logos grow.
- Trust incidents are expensive; budget for transparency and fixes.
