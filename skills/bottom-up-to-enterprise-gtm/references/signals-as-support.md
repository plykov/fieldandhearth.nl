# Usage signals as the sales trigger, and other enterprise-layer notes (paraphrased)

## Supabase: sales that looks like support
- Watch product signals (a database running out of storage or compute). When one fires, the customer gets an automated note offering help.
- The team is paid on how much more those customers grow than a control group that got no contact.
- Reported: customers who talk to a success person grow usage 125% quarter over quarter; those who don't, 25% (CEO, June 2026).
- The company declines large contracts that need custom features ("very painful").
- Precondition: a large base of self-signed customers on usage-priced plans. Don't copy if customers aren't already using the product; otherwise it is cold outreach with a friendlier name.
- Six companies in the series run the bottom-up-to-enterprise pattern.

## Hugging Face: enterprise after grassroots adoption
Individuals and teams pay small subscriptions; companies pay for compute; enterprises pay for a private, secure version (private hosting, on-prem, audit logs, compliance). Precondition: broad adoption inside companies first plus real security. Don't copy if nobody is using you bottom-up yet.

## Design questions to add to the workflow
- Which in-product signals predict readiness for a bigger plan?
- Who gets the automated message and what help do they get?
- How is the team measured (lift versus control rather than bookings)?
- What do we refuse to build for a single large customer?
- Where does the self-serve ceiling sit and what happens above it?

## Contrast: Rogo
A trust-led engine sells to conservative buyers through marquee names, forward-deployed insiders and compliance first; it does not rely on individual bottom-up adoption.
