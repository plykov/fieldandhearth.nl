# Hook mechanics: layering and the 0-3 score

Source: a second analysis (850 top-decile posts from a curated set of expert creators, 40+ variables per post, language-model-enriched). The shares below describe what those creators did, not what causes success. There is no baseline of ordinary posts, so use them as a menu of ingredients.

## What the top hooks contained (share of hooks in that set)
| Ingredient | Share | Why it plausibly works |
|---|---|---|
| A number or statistic | ~62% | Specificity reads as credibility and invites "how?" |
| Quotation marks (a quoted line or dialogue) | ~43% | Drama, realism, overheard-conversation feel |
| A question or how-to framing | ~14% | Opens a loop the reader wants to close |
| Starts with "I" or "My" | ~26-28% | Personal angle first, pivot to the reader later |
| Starts with "You" | ~3% | Rare: top creators usually earn the "you" in the body |
| Visible list or bullets in the preview | ~10% | Signals a skimmable payoff |
| Exclamation marks | ~4% | Factual and curious beat hype |
Median preview length was about 130 characters (roughly three short lines). That is shorter than the ~200-character cutoff, so aim for 130 as a target and treat 200 as the hard ceiling.

## Layering
Most strong hooks combine tactics. In the set, about 95% used at least two and about 67% used three. Examples of layers: personal claim plus number plus a puzzle ("I did X in 26 months. The odd part: one rep ran it 2,000 times"); name-drop plus negation plus a specific goal; a quoted client line plus a question.

## Mechanics score (0-3)
Give one point each for:
1. **Specific number or concrete fact** in the first two lines.
2. **Open loop** (question, how-to, withheld detail, contrarian claim).
3. **Human or dramatic element** (first-person stake, quoted line, named person, vivid moment).
Aim for 2 or 3. A 3 that is dishonest is worse than a 1 that is true.

## Voice notes
Short sentences, fragments on separate lines, plain slang. Mild profanity or bluntness appeared in some top hooks. Use it only if it is the user's real voice and fits their audience; it attracts some buyers and repels others.

## Honesty rules
- The number must be real and sourced from the user. Never invent a metric to raise the score.
- Famous-name hooks ("You are not [famous founder]") need the body to deliver the specific alternative they promise.
- The body must pay off the hook (see `linkedin-post-templates`, body structures).
