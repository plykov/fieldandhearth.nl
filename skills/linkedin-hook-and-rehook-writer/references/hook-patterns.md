# Hook and rehook patterns

All examples are illustrative paraphrases.

## 1. Curiosity hooks
Create an information gap the reader wants to close.
- **Surprising question:** "Did you know [surprising fact]?" The reader wonders whether they are making the mistake.
- **Teaser statement:** "I recently discovered [a result-bearing shortcut]." Words that imply new or insider knowledge (discovered, secret, what nobody tells you) recur in top openers.
- **Cliffhanger intro (in medias res):** open at the tense moment, then explain. "It was 2 AM when the phone rang."

## 2. Problem-solution hooks
Name the reader's pain, imply relief.
- **Question:** "Struggling to [outcome] despite [effort]?" Often pair with a second line that hints a solution exists.
- **Bold problem statement / myth bust:** "[Common belief] is not what you think." or "[Concept A] is NOT [Concept B]." It challenges an assumption and promises clarity.
- **How-to with a twist:** "How to [result] in [short time] without [obstacle]." Or the personal version, "How I [result]."

## 3. Personal story hooks
Connect first, teach second.
- **Transformation:** "From [low point] to [outcome]: what changed." A compressed journey promises lessons.
- **Emotive confession:** "I almost quit [thing] last year." Time anchors and specifics add realism.
- **Quote or dialogue:** open on one line someone said, out of context, so the reader taps for the scene.

Common thread: one clear idea, specific, often addressed to a defined reader.

## 4. Annotated pairs
**A. Strategy reveal.** Hook: "I found a post format that beat my usual reach." Rehook: "It is not the usual advice. Reach rose several times over. Here it is:" Works because it promises value, distances itself from generic advice, quantifies the win and ends on a colon.

**B. Myth bust.** Hook: "An editorial calendar is not a content strategy." Rehook: define the calendar in one line, hint that strategy is something else. Works because it gives half the answer and the reader wants the other half.

**C. Story to insight.** Hook: "I almost quit my business in 2022." Rehook: "Burnout is real and I hit a wall. Here is what pulled me back:" Works because it is personal, universal for the audience and promises a way out.

## 5. Line-break and preview tactics
- The feed cuts after about 200 characters or 3 lines on text-only posts. With an image attached, it shows fewer lines, so write an even punchier opener.
- Put a short first line, then a second line that completes the thought, so the cut lands at suspense.
- A very short hook ("I failed.") works when the next line gives context.
- Split a sentence across lines 2 and 3 if that makes the tap feel necessary.
- Use all-caps for one word of emphasis, since plain posts have no bold.
- Preview your post before publishing, or count ~250 characters.

## 6. Common failures
- Slow start: context before the interesting moment.
- Vague hook ("Some thoughts on leadership").
- Strong line 1, flat lines 2 to 3.
- Hook that promises more than the post delivers.
- Jargon or buzzwords in the opener.
