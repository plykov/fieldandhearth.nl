# Archetypes and psychological triggers

## Contents

## 1. Archetypes
| Archetype | Signature move | Best formats | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Educator | Teaches a method in clear steps | How-to, listicle, case study with results | Generic tips without a point of view |
| Contrarian | Challenges a common belief, backs it up | Hot take, myth bust, "worst advice" list | Cheap outrage; claims you cannot support |
| Mentor | Shares lessons and mistakes with warmth | Personal story, lessons learned, Q&A | Becoming vague or self-focused |
| Thought leader | Frames the field, coins and owns ideas | Opinion with data, trend analysis, named frameworks | Abstract talk with no proof |
Most creators blend two. Choose by temperament and by what the buyer needs to believe before hiring.

## 2. Triggers
| Trigger | In practice | Honest use |
|---|---|---|
| Authority and social proof | Lead with a real result or credential; cite how many people used a resource | Only verified figures; no padding |
| Reciprocity | Give real value freely, including templates and frameworks | Give substance, not teasers |
| Pain and aspiration | Name the reader's frustration, then show a way out (problem, agitate, solution) | Do not exaggerate fear to sell |
| Novelty / FOMO | Share real experiments or new approaches | Do not call something unprecedented if it is not |
| Vulnerability | Admit mistakes and doubts, then show what you learned | True stories only; do not stage failure |
| Narrative transportation | Tell a story the reader can step into; use "you" in scenarios | Keep the story accurate |
| Polarization | State a firm view about ideas | Argue the idea, respect people |

## 3. Positioning moves
- **Signature voice:** pick one tone (witty, analytical, warm, blunt) and keep it. Familiarity lowers the trust barrier before the first call.
- **Show, do not tell:** publish the diagnostic you would give a client.
- **Values as filter:** repeated statements of principle (for example profit over vanity metrics) attract buyers who share them.
- **Own your vocabulary:** a named framework implies a system behind your work. Name it only when it is real.
- **Topic discipline:** each creator in the study owned a narrow cluster of topics. Pillars beat variety.
