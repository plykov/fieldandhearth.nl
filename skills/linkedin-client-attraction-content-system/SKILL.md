# LinkedIn client-attraction content system

Reach is not revenue. The creators studied (850 top-decile posts) mostly used LinkedIn as a trust-building and pre-selling channel: free, useful posts about the topics their buyers care about, with light calls to action that move warm readers into conversation. This skill builds that system around a specific offer and buyer.

## Workflow

1. **Anchor on the offer and buyer.** Ask: who pays, for what outcome, at what price band, and what do they worry about and want? Without this, content drifts into generic virality.
2. **Pick 3 to 4 content pillars** where the buyer's problems meet the user's real expertise (for example "outbound that works", "pricing for services"). Staying inside these pillars is what lets the audience connect your name to a need.
3. **Choose a primary archetype and a secondary one** from `references/archetypes-and-triggers.md` (educator, contrarian, mentor, thought leader). It sets voice and the kinds of posts to lean on.
4. **Build the proof inventory.** List true, usable proof: results with numbers, client stories (with permission), credentials, audits done, mistakes learned from. Social proof is only as good as it is real.
5. **Design the CTA ladder** (below) and match each post's CTA to its job.
6. **Map the path from comment to client** with `references/conversion-path.md`: who gets a DM, what is sent, when a call is offered.
7. **Draft a 90-day plan:** weekly rhythm (for example two teaching posts, one story, one opinion or mindset post, one occasional offer), a monthly review, and the metrics from `linkedin-posting-strategy-and-metrics`.
8. Write the posts with `linkedin-post-templates` and `linkedin-hook-and-rehook-writer`.

## CTA ladder (lightest to heaviest)
| Rung | Example | Use for | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Discussion prompt | "What is your take? Have you seen this?" | Most posts. Builds visibility and relationships | Ask a question you care about, then reply to answers |
| 2. Soft follow | "I share one of these each week." | End of strong value posts | Rare and low-key; the value earns the follow |
| 3. Comment-to-get | "Comment WORD and I will send the template." | A genuinely useful asset, occasionally | Deliver to everyone; say what follows; see guardrails |
| 4. Direct link | "Free breakdown here: link" | Warm moments or when reach is already strong | Links can reduce reach; label it free and useful |
| 5. DM invitation | "DM me if you are stuck on X." | End of a case study aimed at a specific buyer | Only when the post proves you can solve X |
| 6. Soft sell inside a story | "A client closed $X with our template." | Case studies | Real client, real number, permission |
Do not run a hard pitch in a feed post; none of the top posts studied did.

## Principles that make it work
- **Give before asking:** teach real, non-obvious things for free. Reciprocity builds goodwill for later offers.
- **Show, do not claim, expertise:** mini-consultations in public are the proof.
- **Filter by worldview:** a clear point of view (even a mildly polarizing one) attracts the right buyers and repels poor fits. Be polarizing about ideas, never about people.
- **Name your method** if you truly use one, so your thinking is memorable and attributable.
- **Consistency:** a steady rhythm signals reliability, which buyers read as how you will treat their project.
- **Community:** reply to commenters and connect them to each other; the audience becomes a network around you.

## Guardrails
- Use only true numbers, testimonials and credentials. Do not inflate follower counts, revenue, or client results, and get consent before naming clients.
- Comment-gated offers: say what the commenter will receive and any follow-up (such as email sign-up). If DM limits stop you, post a direct link and say so.
- No fake scarcity, manufactured controversy for its own sake, or attacks on named individuals.
- Do not send unsolicited sales DMs to people who only asked for a freebie; ask permission before pitching.
- Check any claim a post makes about results; avoid guarantees.
- The source cohort is a curated set of successful creators with commercial motives. It shows what they do, not what guarantees clients. Read `references/evidence-notes.md`.
